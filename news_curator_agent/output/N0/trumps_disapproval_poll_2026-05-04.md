# Trump Disapproval Reaches Record High in Latest Poll

In a Washington Post-ABC News-Ipsos poll released Friday, the president’s disapproval rating surged to 62%, the highest level recorded across his two terms in office. The poll found that 37% of respondents approved of Trump’s performance, signaling a deepening public backlash as the 2026 midterms approach [1]. The survey was conducted April 24-28 among a representative sample of adults nationwide [3].

The poll’s numbers come as Americans expressed growing concerns about the handling of multiple crises, with Republicans and independents likewise aligning against the president on major issues. Notably, 66% of respondents disapproved of Trump’s management of the war in Iran, while 76% disapproved of his approach to the cost of living, underscoring broad dissatisfaction across the electorate [2].

Analysts say the new figure represents the steepest fall in Trump’s public support across both terms, intensifying pressure on the Republican message ahead of November elections. While the disapproval rating has hovered near historic highs in recent months, the 62% figure marks a new all-time low in the two-term presidency’s polling history, according to the survey cited by multiple outlets [1].

The results arrive as Trump remains a central figure in a crowded political landscape, with primary battles and general-election strategy increasingly influenced by public sentiment about national security, inflation, and the administration’s foreign policy posture. Observers caution that polling snapshots can shift quickly with developments in policy, court cases, or international events, so the numbers warrant careful interpretation in coming weeks as campaigns intensify [3].

The disparate but corroborating coverage from outlets including The Guardian and Forbes underscores the poll’s significance: Trump’s disapproval rating is widely described as a record high, while marginal shifts in approval and issue-specific attitudes receive close scrutiny from political analysts and reporters alike [1][2].

Contextual notes from analysts emphasize that the record-high disapproval reflects concerns about leadership direction and the electorate’s perspective on ongoing crises, a narrative likely to influence fundraising and messaging in the weeks ahead. While not every demographic or region is uniformly aligned, the broad trend toward higher disapproval suggests a challenging environment for the incumbent party as voters weigh economic conditions and foreign-policy commitments [3].
